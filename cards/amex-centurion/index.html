Is the American Express Centurion Card worth its $5,000 annual fee?

Short answer: no combination of the Amex Centurion Card’s credits covers its $5,000 fee; all 3 together come to $1,249. The remaining $3,751 has to come from status, lounges, perks and points. The catch is cadence: $1,000 of the $1,249 advertised arrives in monthly, quarterly or half-year chunks that expire unused.

The arithmetic

$1,249 advertised. $5,000 to cover.

Even if you reached every one of the 3 credits, they would cover $1,249 of the $5,000 fee. The remaining $3,751 has to come from status, lounges, perks and the points the card earns, all valued below.

Of the $1,249: $1,000 arrives in monthly, quarterly or half-year chunks that do not roll over, so that credit needs matching spend on schedule, every period, or the money is gone; $1,000 pays nothing until you enroll or activate it.

$219 of the advertised value comes from terms the issuer states ambiguously; the catalog marks those unverified and the audit scores them conservatively.

None of this says what you will reach. That takes your own charges: the audit matches every credit against 12 months of real transactions, counts what actually landed on the card toward the fee, and shows the rest as upside.
